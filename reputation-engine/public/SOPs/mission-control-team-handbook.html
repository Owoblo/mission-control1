Mission Control Handbook

How Sales, Booking, Operations, and Crew Connect

This is the practical guide for using Mission Control day to day. It explains what sales owns, what operations owns, what crew sees, and where the system is already live versus where the workflow is still manual.

Core Rule

The quote is the commercial promise to the customer. Price, crew size, truck count, route, and deposit expectation come from the quote. Operations can add execution details, but should not silently change what was sold.

If the quote says 1 truck, operations reserves 1 truck. If it says 2, operations reserves 2.

What Is Live Right Now

Your team needs to know which parts are fully active, which parts are tracked manually inside the system, and which parts still need a later automation pass.

Live in Mission Control

Leads, quotes, accepted-quote protection, booked-job handoff, operations board, crew assignment, truck reservation tracking, readiness checklist, and crew-hour storage are all active.

Manual in Mission Control

Truck reservations are entered into the system, but the actual vendor reservation is still a manual step that ops completes and then records back into the booked job.

Not Live Yet

Automatic rental-vendor booking, full worker roster and availability planning, and payroll-grade per-worker payout automation are still future phases.

Who Does What

Mission Control works best when the handoff is clean. Sales should sell clearly. Operations should execute cleanly. Crew should not be guessing.

Owner

  • Sees all modules and admin tools.
  • Reviews locked quote changes and major exceptions.
  • Monitors conversion, ops health, and job outcomes.

Sales Rep

  • Works inbox, pipeline, lead page, calls, SMS, and quotes.
  • Confirms route, date, scope, and customer expectations.
  • Creates the quote that becomes the promise.

Operations Lead

  • Works the booked-job board.
  • Assigns crew and enters truck reservation details.
  • Closes readiness gaps before move day.

Crew

  • Sees assigned jobs on the crew calendar.
  • Uses route, note, phone, truck count, and schedule details.
  • Focuses on execution, not sales cleanup.

Sales Workflow

Sales owns the customer conversation and the integrity of the quote. If sales puts bad information into the quote, operations inherits a bad handoff.

1
Intake

New activity lands in the CRM

Calls, SMS, email, and forms create or update the lead.

2
Triage

Rep works Inbox or Pipeline

Use Inbox for fast response and Pipeline for follow-up and stage control.

3
Lead Page

Rep confirms move details

Addresses, date, scope, access, notes, and customer context should be cleaned up here.

4
Quote

Rep builds the quote

Truck count, crew size, estimated hours, price, and deposit expectation are set here.

5
Close

Customer accepts and deposit is handled

The quote becomes the customer-facing promise. Accepted/booked quote fields are protected.

6
Handoff

Booked job is created for ops

Booked status, branch, crew brief, truck status, and checklist all seed from this point.

Sales rule Do not verbally sell a truck count that differs from the quote. The quote must match what operations will reserve.

Operations Workflow

Operations starts when the move is booked. The job should leave the operations board with a reserved truck, crew assigned, and no major uncertainty left for move day.

1
Booked Job

Open the booked job in Operations

Review route, branch, quoted crew size, quoted truck count, deposit status, and estimated hours.

2
Truck

Reserve against the quote

Use the quote-derived truck count. Reserve the correct number of trucks and store the details back in Mission Control.

3
Crew

Assign crew and crew lead

Set crew members, crew note, and any important execution instruction before move day.

4
Readiness

Finish the ops checklist

Parking, access, truck details, timing, and dispatch readiness should be recorded before the day starts.

5
Crew View

Crew sees the final plan

The crew calendar becomes the operational view for the team on the ground.

6
Actuals

Record actual hours after the move

Actual hours, actual crew count, notes, and other outcome details should be logged cleanly.

Operations rule Mission Control currently tracks truck reservations. It does not automatically book the rental vendor yet, so ops must complete that vendor step and record the confirmation.

Guardrails

These are the rules that stop the system from drifting into confusion.

Do

  • Treat the quote as the source of truth for truck count and commercial scope.
  • Reserve trucks as soon as the move is booked during busy seasons.
  • Use the operations board to capture reservation number, vendor, pickup, and return details.
  • Check for jobs with `needs booking` or incomplete readiness daily.

Do Not

  • Rewrite quoted commercial fields casually after acceptance or booking.
  • Leave a booked move without a truck status in the system.
  • Leave crew to figure out route or truck expectations on move day.
  • Assume vendor automation exists before it has been built and tested.

Current Limits

This is where the product ends today, so the team uses the right workflow and does not over-trust automation that is not there yet.

Active Today

  • Quote locking on accepted/booked jobs.
  • Ops-only dispatch updates.
  • Truck reservation status and detail tracking.
  • Crew-hour storage on booked jobs.

Future Build Areas

  • Automatic vendor booking logic.
  • Nearest rental location recommendation.
  • Full worker roster and availability model.
  • Payroll-grade worker-hour exports and payout flow.
