Sales Flow

Sales owns the customer conversation from first touch to accepted quote. The lead page is the working file, and the quote is the commercial promise.

Step 1

Inbound source

Calls, SMS, email, and website forms create or update the lead.

Step 2

Inbox triage

Reps review fresh inbound activity in Inbox and claim or continue the conversation.

Step 3

Lead page

Rep confirms route, date, inventory, parking, access, and notes in the main customer file.

Step 4

Quote engine

The system calculates crew size, hours, truck count, route pricing, deposit, and total.

Step 5

Quote sent

The customer receives a quote link and can review the move plan and pricing.

Step 6

Accepted and deposit

Once the quote is accepted and deposit is confirmed, the job moves into booked state.

Step 7

Booked handoff

Booking creates the operations handoff and seeds the dispatch layer for the move.

Operations Flow

Operations owns execution after booking. The operations board turns the sold move into a scheduled truck, an assigned crew, and a move-day-ready job.

Step 1

Booked job appears

The move shows up on the operations board with branch, route, deposit, quoted crew, truck count, and estimated hours.

Step 2

Dispatch setup

Manager or ops lead opens the setup panel and assigns crew, notes, and readiness items.

Step 3

Truck reservation

Ops reserves the truck manually with the vendor, then records pickup, return, status, and reservation number.

Step 4

Readiness check

Ops confirms access, parking, tools, job packet, and final walkthrough status before move day.

Step 5

Crew calendar

Crew sees assigned jobs only, including route, move type, truck count, key items, and dispatch note.

Step 6

Move day

The team executes the move using the agreed plan instead of improvising trucks or scope on the day.

Step 7

Actuals and review

After the move, ops logs actual hours and crew counts and the review flow can be triggered.

Source of Truth Rule

This is the rule that matters most for summer truck planning: operations should reserve the same truck plan that sales sold.

Truck count comes from the quote

The quote is the commercial promise. If the customer was sold 1 truck, operations should reserve 1 truck. If the customer was sold 2 trucks, operations should reserve 2 trucks.

  • The quote owns pricing, crew size, route, and truck count.
  • The booked job stores reservation details against that promise.
  • The dispatch panel should not be used to quietly change what the customer bought.
Current live behavior

When a job is confirmed, Mission Control seeds the booked job with the quoted truck count and marks the reservation status as Needs booking.

Meaning for the team

Sales sells the plan. Operations executes the plan. Crew receives the plan. Everyone works from the same truck count.

Role Map

Different people should work different surfaces. The system is already split that way.

Owner

  • Full access across sales, operations, finance, and team management.
  • Can review quotes, bookings, dispatch, and audit behavior.

Manager

  • Works across sales and operations.
  • Can assign crew, review booked jobs, and oversee readiness.

Sales Rep

  • Lives in Dashboard, Pipeline, Inbox, Quotes, and the Lead page.
  • Owns customer communication and quote creation.

Operations Lead

  • Lives in the Operations board.
  • Owns dispatch setup, truck reservation details, checklist, and crew hours.

Crew

  • Lives in the Crew calendar.
  • Sees assigned jobs and the execution note, not the full CRM.

Key System Objects

Lead The working customer file. Communication, stage, route, notes, and post-booking ops details all live here.
Quote The customer-facing commercial promise. Price, deposit, crew size, route, and truck count live here.
Booked job The lead after confirmation. This is where dispatch, truck reservation, and readiness get managed.

Recommendation for today: reserve trucks immediately after booking, store every reservation detail in Mission Control, and use the dispatch panel as the single operations checklist until vendor automation is built.
