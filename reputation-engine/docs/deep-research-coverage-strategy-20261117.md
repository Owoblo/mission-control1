# Deep research: breadth, depth and relationship timing

Allocation percentages remain initial planning recommendations to evaluate. This document does not start paid workers, increase research caps or activate outreach.

## Recommendation

Organize coverage by canonical municipality and category; select the next research task using existing relationship context, fresh evidence and unresolved gaps. Maintain broad coverage independently of commercial priority. Go horizontal for inventory, vertical for an actionable question, and overlap sources to reconcile identities and uncover omissions. Do not exhaust Windsor before starting the other cities, or exhaust Realtors before commercial and institutional categories receive scheduled coverage.

Reuse docs/coverage-expansion-operating-playbook-20261028.txt and its coverage workqueue. The existing first wave explicitly does not represent the full city/category/source product. Generate later bounded waves from the ledger rather than inventing a second incompatible coverage model. Configured locality labels must resolve to canonical municipalities while retaining neighbourhood aliases and service areas.

## Coverage structure

Use municipality → neighbourhood/grid/corridor → category → source, with stable organization, location and person identities beneath the observations. Keep the existing 28 outreach groups and crosswalk them to NAICS where applicable; do not equate sales categories, zoning codes and economic classifications. Retain uncategorized records for review. Multi-category businesses can have multiple relationship roles without becoming duplicate people.

Track separately: residential property/direct-mail coverage; residential/rental referral partners; commercial businesses and independent shops; corporate accounts and branches; public/institutional accounts and procurement; adjacent service partners. The categories are broad coverage families, not a replacement exhaustive taxonomy. Public agencies and other entities outside business-directory coverage require their own sources.

Each city/category/source cell records source version, exact query/geographic bounds, pagination, last successful check, partial/error reason, candidate counts, verified current entities, existing CRM matches, unresolved identities/routes and next scheduled review. Unknown, searched-zero, blocked and partially collected remain distinct. No eligible source results does not prove no organizations exist.

Use Statistics Canada location counts by municipality and industry as a plausibility benchmark, not a named contact directory or an exact recall denominator. Include employee and non-employee businesses where available and align classification/reference date before comparing. Separate organizations from their locations and contacts. A chain with ten branches may have one central purchasing relationship; a tower may contain many independent tenants.

## Research decision ladder

1. Check full conversation history, preferences, suppressions, existing research, directory identities, saved source pages and Sales ownership.
2. Reuse attributable captured evidence; extract structured public facts directly when practical. Refresh stale evidence with the relevant source before commissioning broad model research.
3. Commission bounded deep research only for a named question that changes an action: which organization occupies this site; who is responsible for facilities or procurement; is this a shared office route; does this listing agent match our contact; what source establishes a current relocation; what is the legitimate professional contact route?
4. Save the answer, evidence URL/date, identity confidence, unresolved conflicts, cost and next action. Reuse it across cities, branches and channels. An unavailable private route is not a reason for endless retries.
5. Recheck context before outreach. A new reply or opt-out supersedes an older research result. Hold complaints, promised meetings, conflicting identities and unsupported service promises for review.

## Scheduling without blind spots

Handle inbound replies and real Sales opportunities independently of discovery quotas. As a starting allocation for a bounded research budget, propose 50% existing contacts with specific gaps or fresh evidence; 30% new entities in priority referral/direct-buyer categories across cities; 20% rotating underserved cities, categories and source families. These are adjustable planning shares, not a demonstrated optimum or a change to live worker limits. Track both time and paid request cost so expensive tasks cannot consume a nominal task quota unnoticed.

Use weighted rotation across municipalities and category families, with an age-based priority increase for unserved cells. Give each enabled city a documented review turn; disclose skipped cities and the next review rather than claiming equal daily execution. Large cities can receive more capacity while smaller cities retain recurring turns. Batch common website/directory work so one brokerage, school board or parent company can resolve multiple local questions.

Market activity changes timing and priority, not whether a relationship remains in the universe. Research listings/open houses and conditional or sold episodes; also commercial leasing, openings, relocations, expansions, renovations, institutional tenders and relevant events. Retain source dates and explicit meanings: an open house does not prove a sale or an imminent move. Use signals privately to prioritize a natural, context-aware message; do not imply personal knowledge or certainty not supported by the evidence.

Suggested initial review cadence: active replies each working day; time-sensitive signal sources daily where feasible; discovery coverage rotation weekly; broader inventory refresh monthly or on provider releases; quiet-relationship review approximately 45–60 days subject to established context and channel policy. A review is not an automatic message, and a new signal does not override a recent-contact hold.

## Physical geography before contact discovery

Treat each configured municipality as an accountable GIS research scope. Start with its official boundary and local zoning or land-use definitions. Overlay parcels, footprints, civic addresses and street context. Use a second address or building source to expose omissions and conflicts. Keep commercial, industrial, mixed-use, institutional and government property in scope, including institutions that sit outside a simple commercial zoning filter.

Preserve separate records for the parcel, building or footprint, each reported civic address and unit, and each dated occupant observation. Keep published unit numbers as recorded and mark unknown units explicitly. Use address, unit and building evidence before joining a business or facility directory to a site. Verify occupancy from official location pages, municipal or licensing sources, school and hospital directories, tenant directories or other attributable evidence. A mapped address, footprint or zoning permission provides physical context. An operator or tenant needs its own evidence.

The [physical evidence ledger](../partnership-system/state/geo-coverage-progress/physical-foundation.json) joins the existing atlas, publisher receipts and saved location evidence for all 107 configured municipalities. The owner coverage email compares that evidence to the day's research by city. Locations without a domain remain part of the mapped footprint. Each missing or restricted layer has a named research action. Keep these tasks active even when the city has usable outreach stock.

Use the existing atlas acquisition queue and site-research coordinator to advance this work. Acquire reviewed sources in resumable municipality turns with request and storage budgets. Keep the exact source scope, pagination and observation receipts. For known sites, retain unresolved buildings, address conflicts and unverified occupants in the existing research queue.
